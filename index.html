Campaign code

How the war works

  • The war table is a board of provinces. Blue is ISA (yours, at the bottom), red is Helghast; the trench is the front line.
  • Your armies are the blue standards (I, II, III...). Each holds up to three platoons, who keep their sergeants and their losses from battle to battle.
  • Your turn: tap an army (or its chip at the bottom). Badges show what it can do: March to a province of yours, Take an empty enemy province, or Fight an enemy army (the odds are shown). Each army acts once a turn. Then press End turn.
  • Battles start as soon as you attack. Fight it yourself on the battle screen, or let the AI fight it. Win and the province is yours; the beaten army falls back, or is lost if it has nowhere to go.
  • The Helghast turn comes next. Pulsing red borders show where they can strike; when they attack you, you choose to fight it or let the AI defend.
  • Supply comes in every turn from the provinces joined to your capital (enemy home ground you hold pays only 1, and a side that has lost ground gets extra). Spend it in the army panel: Refill lost soldiers, Dig in (fight from trenches when attacked, uses the army's turn), or Raise a platoon (up to three an army; tap your capital to raise a new army).
  • The war opens with a ceasefire turn: march into position and dig in; attacks open on turn 2. A side down to two provinces surrenders.
  • Sites add to every battle while held and joined to your capital: the tank works gives you a tank, the power plant artillery, the comms hub an order point, the airfield jetpacks.
  • Defending your own home ground helps, and every few turns the Helghast make a big push. An objective (gold dashed outline) pays extra supply if you take it in time.
  • Victory: take the Helghast capital, or hold the most provinces when the last turn ends. Lose Fort Corvane and the war is lost.
  • Drag (or use the arrow keys) to pan, pinch to zoom, twist to turn the table. Every battle can be replayed from its result.

How to play

  • Two ways to fight (menu, Battle mode): real time as below, or turn-based: the same battle on a grid of tiles. Each platoon and the tank get two actions a turn: move (blue tiles), dash (amber), fire (the hit chance and its reasons are shown, then rolled), overwatch (fires at the first enemy to move in sight) or grenades. A hit kills soldiers, a miss pins (one action next turn). Cover counts unless you flank from the side. Then press End turn and the Helghast move. Objectives score each round; most points after 10 turns wins. The soldiers keep fighting on screen, but only the turn rules decide losses.
  • You command the ISA on the left. The Helghast hold the right.
  • Your units are the chips bottom left (three platoons and the tank). Tap one (or its soldiers on the map) and its orders pop up; then tap the map. Tap an objective or a capturable post to take it, or open ground to move there.
  • Fog of war: you only see what your troops see (the rest is dimmed); orange question marks show where enemies were last seen. Troops lying still in cover are hard to spot until they move or fire, and shots from hiding hit harder. Hold fire (order tray) sets an ambush: the platoon lies low until the enemy is close or it is shot at, then opens up all at once.
  • Flanking: squads face their threat and their cover only works that way (the shaded arc in front of enemy squads while you give orders). Hit them from the side or behind for much more damage and pinning. Pinned squads cannot turn. Pin with heavies, flank with assault or jetpacks.
  • Squad orders: a platoon order is the shared goal for all three squads. When you need one squad somewhere else, pick it in the tray (or tap one of its soldiers), then tap the map (1 point). Rejoin sends it back; the platoon's next order brings everyone back too.
  • Focus fire: with a platoon selected, tap an enemy soldier you can see. All three squads shoot that squad first for 40 seconds (1 point). A red target marks it.
  • While a unit is selected the grid shows cover: green tiles have good cover (trenches, ruins, sandbags), amber some (craters, rubble, high ground).
  • Squads look after themselves: rifles bound between cover, heavies stop and suppress, assault squads flank, engineers cut wire, recon spots from behind.
  • Every order costs an order point (the number top right). They refill over time, faster if you hold the comms post.
  • Hold objectives A, B and C to score. Highest score when the clock runs out wins, or break every enemy platoon.
  • Under heavy fire a squad gets pinned and will not advance. Use cover, smoke, or fall back.
  • Each squad has an ability (grenade, barrage, charge, sandbags, mark) with its own button under the platoon cards. They are free but need time to recharge.
  • Jetpack troopers fly straight over cliffs, wire and trenches (but get no cover in the air). Infiltrators stay hidden until they fire or an enemy gets close; their first shots hit hardest. A recon Mark exposes them.
  • Each side has one tank (its own card). Tap the card, then the ground to drive there (1 point). Its main gun shells groups of infantry and the enemy tank; its machine gun sprays anyone close. Overwatch fires an aimed heavy shell. Infantry close behind their tank are covered, and it flattens wire.
  • Rifles barely scratch a tank. Hurt it with heavies, grenades, artillery, the arc tower, jetpack dives, your own tank, and engineers: with an enemy tank close, their ability becomes a breaching charge. A knocked out tank stays as a wreck you can hide behind.
  • Maps (menu): Trenches, an Industrial site or a Ruined city. On the built-up maps walls block movement and sight and shelter anyone beside them; artillery, grenades and tank shells blow holes in them.
  • High ground gives range and accuracy. Cliffs can only be climbed at ramps, so hills make chokepoints.
  • Time slows right down while a platoon is selected, so you can think. Tap the card again to let go.
  • Tapped the wrong spot? For 5 seconds after an order you can tap again to change it for free (the card counts down).
  • While a platoon is selected a faint grid shows; move orders snap to a tile, and its three squads take that tile and the ones above and below.
  • Reinforce (2 points) sends up to five fresh soldiers from your lines to refill the selected platoon's weakest squad.
  • Every squad has a medic (small cross). Soldiers hit by bullets go down for 25 seconds (a red cross with a shrinking ring) and a medic can get them back up; blasts and shells kill outright.
  • Stance also sets what pinned squads do: Cautious crawl back to cover, Aggressive crawl forward onto their objective and get up sooner, and their platoon mates close in further to help.
  • A platoon that loses too many men breaks and runs back to its own lines. The first time, it regroups there and rejoins the fight; break it again and it is gone.
  • Reading the fight: the bar by the score is troops left on each side. A red arc on your squad points at who is shooting it. Red arrows at the screen edge point to your platoons in trouble off screen: tap one to look. Tap a message with › to jump to where it happened.
  • Drag to pan, pinch to zoom, twist two fingers to turn the map (the compass turns it back to north; the tilted look keeps north up), tap the minimap to jump. Press and hold anything to see what it does. On a keyboard, the space bar pauses and resumes and the arrow keys move the view.
